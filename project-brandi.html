BrandI

An AI-powered content strategy assistant that helps creators plan, create and schedule posts while staying true to their brand.

Duration
12 weeks, Sep 10 to Nov 20
My contribution
AI brand-tone analysis, personalization and recommendation logic
Tools
Figma, Maze, Google Forms, Miro
Platform
iOS, on the App Store

Overview

Social media moves fast. Platforms shift, trends change by the week, and creators still need to publish consistent, high-quality content. BrandI puts planning, creating and scheduling in one place, with the creator's brand identity guiding every post.

Problem and objectives

Creators struggle to organize, plan and produce high-quality content because tools are not tailored to them. The result is inconsistency across platforms.

Objective: help creators stay consistent by aligning every post with their brand identity and organizing posts effectively. Primary audience: influencers and small business owners.

90%of creators struggle to maintain consistency
33%need time management tools
12weeks, five design thinking stages

A competitive analysis of Buffer, Hootsuite and Later showed scheduling, analytics and multi-account support are well covered. Only Buffer allows personalization, which is where BrandI differs.

My role and contributions

I designed an AI-powered system that analyzes brand tone and translates it into structured content strategies, and built the system logic for content personalization and AI-driven recommendation workflows.

Tools and approach

Design Thinking: understand, define, ideate, design, test. Research combined interviews, surveys, empathy mapping, personas, user journeys and usability testing.

  • Figma
  • Maze
  • Google Forms
  • Miro
  • SwiftUI

What sets it apart

  • Analyzes brand style and tone for real-time feedback.
  • AI-driven suggestions keep content on brand.
  • Voice commands speed up content creation.
  • Schedules are visualized across platforms.
  • Posts can be shared directly to Instagram and TikTok.

Key decisions and lessons

  • Usability testing changed the Agenda. We improved color contrast, simplified content display, and grouped posts and events by date instead of one card at a time.
  • Differentiate on personalization. The competitor review pointed to brand-aware suggestions as the gap.
  • Research shaped the pain points. Time pressure, shifting algorithms and tool fragmentation became the three problems to solve.
