Wasilah

A fintech product concept and business analysis case study for early-stage founders who cannot easily get a company card.

Duration
Two months
My role
UX research and business analysis
Tools
Figma, Miro, Notion, Google Forms
Audience
Micro-startup founders in tech

Overview

Wasilah helps startup founders who struggle to get a pre-paid company card quickly. They document work-related expenses paid from a personal card, see where the money goes, and cut manual work.

Problem and objectives

Early-stage founders lack easy, fast access to corporate pre-paid cards. That leads to manual documentation and complicated budgeting, which costs time, raises the risk of errors and pulls attention away from the product.

Objective: a cost-effective, simple automated financial tool for micro-businesses at an early stage.

My role and contributions

I owned the research and analysis.

  • User research and empathy mapping
  • Persona creation and user stories
  • Market analysis
  • Problem identification and hypothesis formulation

Research

I interviewed four small business owners, then four tech founders, mapped each with an empathy map, and ran a survey to measure the pain.

75%find it hard to obtain a bank card for work expenses
50%use a personal card for company expenses
59%have no card for work expenses

Founders also said getting a company card can take three months or more.

Key decisions and lessons

  • Two hypotheses failed. I started on product waste. Owners already had shelf-life alerts and products arrived categorized, so both guesses were invalid. Overstock traced back to weak financial forecasting, which redirected the project to finance.
  • One industry, not all. Daftra and Qoyod serve many industries and overwhelm tiny teams, while Salla's focus on online stores worked well. So I focused on tech startups.
